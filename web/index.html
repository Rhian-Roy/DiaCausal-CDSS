Why causal? (the worked example)

In old records doctors gave different drugs to different patients, so a plain comparison is unfair.

High starting HbA1c300 patients, −1.2100 patients, −1.0−0.2
Lower starting HbA1c100 patients, −0.6300 patients, −0.5−0.1
Everyone pooled (naive)−1.05−0.625−0.425

Like with like, weighting both groups equally: 0.5 × (−0.2) + 0.5 × (−0.1) = −0.15. The naive answer is about three times too big, only because sicker patients got SGLT2i more often (confounding by indication). DiaCausal corrects this with propensity weighting and doubly robust estimation, and says “insufficient evidence” when too few similar patients got an option. Illustrative numbers.

Evidence from licence-cleared sources

The RAG part of DiaCausal: it finds the passages of approved documents that best answer a question and shows each one with its citation. It never writes its own answer and never shows doses. Your question stays on this device.

How the evidence search (RAG) works
  1. Licence gate. Only sources whose licence allows copying, confirmed by a team member, are read.
  2. Chunks. Each document is cut into passages of about 400 words that never cross a section, each labelled with source, version, section and page.
  3. Two searches. BM25 finds exact words (“eGFR”); a vector search finds similar wording (TF-IDF today, a medical embedding model in October).
  4. Fusion. Reciprocal rank fusion merges the two rankings; the best 5 passages are kept.
  5. Abstain or cite. If nothing matches well enough the answer is “insufficient evidence”; passages with dose text are withheld.
  6. Next (October). Evidence fusion with the causal engine, and an LLM that explains the result citing only these passages.

About DiaCausal

What it is

A decision-support research prototype. For one adult with type 2 diabetes already on metformin it compares an SGLT2 inhibitor, a DPP-4 inhibitor and a sulfonylurea. First it removes unsafe options with cited drug-label rules. Then it estimates each remaining option's 6-month HbA1c change with a 95% range, or says “insufficient evidence”. The clinician decides.

What it is not

  • Not a marketed medical device and not for unsupervised clinical use.
  • Not trained on real patients: every number comes from an India-calibrated synthetic cohort.
  • Never shows drug doses.

Your privacy

Everything is calculated on this device. The patient details and questions you type are never sent anywhere or stored.

Accounts

Anyone can ask for an account. Each person sets up an authenticator app, and the team's admin approves the account before Try it and Evidence open. The account service (Supabase, Mumbai region) keeps only your name, email, role and approval date. The Results, Learn and About pages are open to all.
