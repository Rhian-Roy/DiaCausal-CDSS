How the evidence search works
  1. Licence gate. Only sources whose licence allows copying, confirmed by a team member, are read.
  2. Passages. Each document is cut into passages of about 400 words that never cross a section, each labelled with source, version, section and page.
  3. Two searches. BM25 finds exact words (“eGFR”); a vector search finds similar wording (TF-IDF today).
  4. Fusion. Reciprocal rank fusion merges the two rankings; the best 5 passages are kept.
  5. Abstain or cite. If nothing matches well enough the result is “insufficient evidence”; passages with dosing text are withheld.
  6. Checked first. The same seven input checks as the server run on your search before anything is searched; a blocked search is not kept.

Intro

DiaCausal is a decision-support research prototype for one decision: an adult with type 2 diabetes is already taking metformin, their HbA1c is still above target, and the doctor is choosing a second medicine. It compares three options — an SGLT2 inhibitor, a DPP-4 inhibitor and a sulfonylurea — for that one patient.

Problem statement

Average results from trials do not say how a particular patient is likely to respond, and looking at which patients did well on which drug in routine records mixes up the effect of the drug with the reasons it was chosen. A tool that simply predicts “who does well” can therefore point the wrong way. It also needs to show how sure it is and where its statements come from.

Solution

  • Safety rules first. Cited rules remove options that are unsafe for this patient before anything is estimated.
  • Causal estimate. A causal model estimates each remaining option's 6-month HbA1c change for this patient, with a 95% interval, correcting for why patients received each drug.
  • Evidence. Passages from licence-cleared sources are retrieved and cited with source, version and section.
  • Abstain when unsure. If the patient is unlike anyone the model has seen, it says “insufficient evidence” instead of guessing.

Workflow

  1. Step 1PatientAge, duration, HbA1c, eGFR, BMI, history, budget.
  2. Step 2Safety rulesCited rules remove unsafe options first.
  3. Step 3Causal estimate6-month HbA1c change per option, with a 95% interval.
  4. Step 4EvidenceCited passages from licence-cleared sources.
  5. Step 5Clinician decidesDiaCausal shows the comparison. You choose.

Cautions

  • Decision support only. DiaCausal never chooses for you.
  • Check every value in the patient details before comparing. A typing error changes the estimate.
  • Do not type names, Aadhaar, phone, PAN or email.
  • In an emergency, follow your emergency protocol. DiaCausal will not answer.
  • Doses are never shown. Use the product label and your formulary.
  • “What drives this estimate” comes from SHAP: it shows which patient details move the estimate in the model, not what causes the effect, and with many details checked an occasional driver can be a chance finding.

Limitations

  • All estimates come from a synthetic India-calibrated cohort. They have not been validated on real patients.
  • Only three options and one primary outcome (6-month HbA1c), with hypoglycaemia risk, weight change and monthly cost as secondary measures.
  • The evidence search covers a small set of licence-cleared sources (a few dozen passages).
  • Evidence level is never “High”, because the data are synthetic.
  • Not covered: type 1 diabetes, pregnancy, under-18s, starting insulin.

Advantages

  • Unsafe options are removed by cited, deterministic rules — not by a language model.
  • Every estimate carries a 95% interval, and the tool withholds an answer rather than guess.
  • Every clinical statement links to its source, version and section.
  • Every request is logged and versioned, without patient values in the log.
  • Works on a desktop monitor and on a phone.

What it is, and what it is not

A decision-support research prototype. For one adult with type 2 diabetes already on metformin it compares an SGLT2 inhibitor, a DPP-4 inhibitor and a sulfonylurea. It removes unsafe options with cited drug-label rules first, then estimates each remaining option's 6-month HbA1c change with a 95% interval, or says “insufficient evidence”. The clinician decides.

  • Not a marketed medical device and not for unsupervised clinical use.
  • Not trained on real patients: every number comes from an India-calibrated synthetic cohort.
  • Never shows drug doses.

Your privacy and accounts

Everything is calculated on this device. The patient details and questions you type are not sent anywhere or stored.

Anyone can ask for an account. Each person sets up an authenticator app, and the team's admin approves the account before Patient Details and Investigate open. The account service (Supabase, Mumbai region) keeps only your name, email, role and approval date. Analysis, Guide and About are open to all.

Use it like an app on your phone

  1. Open this page in Safari (iPhone) or Chrome (Android).
  2. Tap Share (iPhone) or the ⋮ menu (Android).
  3. Choose Add to Home Screen, then Add.

It then opens full-screen from its icon and keeps working without internet.
