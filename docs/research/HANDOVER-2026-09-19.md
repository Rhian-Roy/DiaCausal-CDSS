## Member B — licence table (`RAG/sources.csv`)

What changed from earlier plans:
- **RSSDI 2022 is NOT openly licensed any more.** Its PMC permission was tied to the
  COVID-19 pandemic declaration. Use the **RSSDI-ESI 2020** version in the Indian Journal
  of Endocrinology and Metabolism instead: that journal published under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0
  in 2020. Confirm the licence line on the article's own PMC page (PMID 32699774).
- **NICE**: using it for AI needs written permission and a licence. Excluded.
- **IDF 2025**: "no part may be reproduced" without written permission. Excluded unless IDF agrees.
- **ICMR STW and ICMR 2018**: copyright DHR/ICMR, no reuse licence stated. Cite only, and
  email for permission.
- Only these are cleared to ingest today: **WHO 2018** (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO), **FDA safety
  communications**, and (after your check) **RSSDI-ESI 2020**.

Your job: open every URL, confirm each licence yourself, fill `checked_by`, send the two
permission emails (ICMR/DHR, IDF), and delete X01–X03 from `RAG/`.

## Member D — guard rules (`rules.v1.json`)

- Built from the LDNOOBW English and Hindi lists (CC BY 4.0 — attribution file included).
- I removed words that are clinical or descriptive, e.g. body parts, "sex", "urine",
  "cialis/viagra", and Hindi "tatti" (stool). Also "cum" (Indian English "-cum-") and
  "xx"/"xxx" (our designs use ₹XXX placeholders).
- **Worked example:** the unedited list blocked 5 of 6 real clinical questions; the edited
  one passes all 22 must-pass cases.
- Proof: `python3 shared/guard_rules/reference_guard.py` → **47/47 cases match**.
- Negation and history are handled: "not pregnant", "no prior DKA", "seizures as a child",
  "past severe hypoglycaemia" do NOT trigger scope or emergency notices.
- Known limitation (on purpose): any 10-digit number starting 6–9 is treated as a phone number.
- Your job: read the blocked list once (it is offensive — that's its purpose), add
  Marathi/Hinglish terms you know, and check the two glucose thresholds against their
  papers (54 mg/dL — IHSG 2017; 600 mg/dL — HHS consensus 2024).
